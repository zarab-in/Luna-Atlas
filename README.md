## About the Project
- **Project Name**: Luna Atlas
- **Team Name**: zarabin
- **Nasa Space Apps 2026 Challenge**: CLPS Lunar Mission Browser

### Summary
Luna Atlas turns complex lunar terrain, illumination, and Earth-visibility data into an interactive mission-planning tool for answering one deceptively simple question: 
*“Where and when can we operate on the Moon?”*

Features include:
1. A central dashboard for exploring current, past, and upcoming CLPS lunar missions.
2. Presents complex NASA datasets into an interactive landing page; a format suitable for researchers, teachers, and students.
3. All assets and NASA datasets are fully cached locally for complete offline functionality.

### The NSAC Challenge Summary
Missions to the Moon’s south pole face extreme lighting and communication constraints, and planning them takes careful coordination between science and engineering teams. Tools for determining Sun and Earth positions at candidate landing sites exist, but their complicated interfaces make it difficult to evaluate critical information with efficiency. 

Your challenge is to create an intuitive tool or application that lets mission planners, educators, and the public compare landing sites and dates quickly, visualizing Sun and Earth positions relative to the horizon to assess power generation potential and direct-to-Earth communication windows.

### The Solution
Luna Atlas is an interactive mission‑planning tool that visualizes Sun and Earth positions over lunar terrain, so planners can compare landing sites and dates in seconds instead of hours.

### NASA Dataset Integration
The website directly uses and visualizes NASA’s CLPS Manifest Data, Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO) Lunar Digital Elevation Models (DEM), and Apollo/CLPS landing site coordinate datasets.

NASA datasets serve as the primary foundation for all landing site markers, payload specs, and surface coordinate renderings rather than secondary surface-level decorations.
