The Problem & Solution
Problem: At the lunar south pole, calculating 72-hour solar exposure and Earth-direct communications means juggling fragmented, complex tools while the clock ticks down.
Solution: We unify line-of-sight, power, and comms analysis into one instant platform—giving mission controllers real-time answers when every second counts.

Why does it matter ?
Comms Blackouts: From the lunar south pole, Earth is visible only about half of each ~14‑day lunar month, and always low on the horizon.
Solar Volatility: Some polar areas swing between continuous darkness and sunlight depending on the time of year; so a good landing site can be unusable for weeks or months at a time.
Tracking Blindspots: Without direct line‑of‑sight to Earth, traditional ground‑station tracking becomes difficult or impossible during surface operations.

Mission success at the south pole depends on two simple questions:
Where can we land, and when can we operate?
Luna Atlas is built to answer exactly that.

How does Luna Atlas work?
Luna Atlas is an interactive mission‑planning tool that visualizes Sun and Earth positions over lunar terrain, so planners can compare landing sites and dates in seconds instead of hours.
INPUT: Planners pick a candidate landing site and a date range.
COMPUTE: Uses NASA lunar data to calculate Sun elevation, lighting duration, and Earth visibility above the local horizon.
OUTPUT: Shows color‑coded timelines and horizon plots: green when power and comms are good, red when they’re not.
